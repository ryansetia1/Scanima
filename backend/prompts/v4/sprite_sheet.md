SURFACE MARKS — OMIT, NEVER REPLACE
The reference photograph may carry brand logos, wordmarks, swooshes,

lettering, or other emblem-like graphics. Treat every such mark as absent from

OBJECT-TO-CREATURE TRANSFORMATION — MOST IMPORTANT
The result must look like a real creature born from the object, not an object
with generic eyes and arms pasted onto it. Preserve the object's silhouette
logic and strongest physical features, but simplify tiny surface details.

The object's parts do NOT all need to become literal anatomy. Biological eyes,
mouths, teeth, paws, claws, or limbs are allowed when they make the character
more expressive. Object parts may instead become armor plates only when rigid,
brow shapes, crests without symbols, scales, tails, weapons, or accessories.

Choose a body plan that naturally follows this object's geometry and the visual
transformation above. It may be bipedal, quadrupedal, serpentine, shelled,
floating, winged, many-legged, or amorphous. Do not default every object to the
same quadruped, insect, mascot, robot, or cyborg anatomy.

Cute qualities should come from expression, posture, and behavior. Fierce
qualities should come from silhouette, anatomy, and pose. Keep shapes simple,
readable, and strong at mobile-game size.

COLOR
The object's dominant colors occupy most of the creature. Use darker versions
for shadows, lighter versions for highlights, and at most 1–2 compact accent
colors for personality or elemental details. Accent colors must follow anatomy
or material boundaries, never form an emblem or logo-like isolated mark. Do not
automatically make every creature black, neon, or rainbow-colored.

CHARACTER CONSISTENCY

proportions, head/body relationship, facial structure, eye design, limb count,
palette, material finish, accessories, anatomy, and object-derived signature
features. Only pose, expression, restrained effects, and damage state may
change.
