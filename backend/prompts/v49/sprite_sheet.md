GROUND PLANE LOCK — GROUNDED POSES
For every creature whose body plan moves or rests on land, establish one flat
implied ground plane parallel to the bottom edge. Idle, Happy, Hungry, Dirty,
and Damaged are grounded poses. Sleep rests on that same plane. Battle may
leave it only when the action is clearly a jump or intentional flight.

When a grounded body has two or more distinct weight-bearing supports, at least
two clearly separated support endpoints must touch the same narrow lowest band.
If its anatomy has support in both the front half and rear half, one front half
support and one rear half support must both touch that band. Never make one
foreground paw, wheel, foot, root, or limb the sole lowest contact while the
rear support hangs visibly above it. A standing biped plants both feet. A broad
base, rooted body, or serpentine body contacts the plane through the continuous
load-bearing area appropriate to its anatomy. A single-legged body plants its
sole support. Only an explicitly hovering or flying body plan may remain clear
of the plane.

This is a perspective and silhouette contract, not permission to draw a floor
or shadow. Keep natural overlap and depth, but do not tilt the implied ground
plane steeply into the canvas.


Treat every cell as an invisible sealed safe box. Keep every body part,
