GLOBAL STYLE LOCK — IDENTICAL FOR EVERY SCANIMA
2D Japanese anime creature character design with the clear silhouettes, broad
character range, and expressive readability of late-1990s monster games, while
remaining an entirely original character that does not copy or closely
resemble an existing franchise design.

Polished game character illustration. Clean confident anime linework, moderately
bold dark graphic contours, simplified stylized forms, strong readable
silhouette, object-led proportions, slightly exaggerated anatomy where anatomy
exists, and an expressive anime creature face.

Use flat base colors, crisp 2–3 level cel shading, hard-edged anime shadows,
small controlled highlights, and minimal gradients. The image must look clearly
hand-illustrated in 2D, never rendered as CGI.

Techno-organic, robotic, armored, wired, or mechanical details are appropriate
ONLY when the photographed object is itself electronic or mechanical. Organic,
ceramic, glass, fabric, wood, paper, food, and plant objects must retain their
own material language instead of becoming robots or cyborgs.

CHARACTER RANGE — FOLLOW THE OBJECT
Express the character direction consistently through silhouette, proportions,
face, posture, and movement. Do not default to fierce, angry, masculine, cute,
or childlike. Do not invent bows, eyelashes, muscles, facial hair, clothing,
symbols, or gender-coded accessories unsupported by the object.

SURFACE MARKS — OMIT, NEVER REPLACE
Treat every brand logo, wordmark, swoosh, trademarked stripe arrangement,
printed tag, model number, readable letter, or emblem-like graphic in the
reference as absent. Draw plain object-faithful material in its place.

Never invent a replacement emblem, sigil, rune, badge, chevron, swoosh, shield,
crest icon, isolated stripe motif, readable text, or logo-like symbol. Surface
interest comes only from real material cues such as weave, grain, glaze, seams,
natural speckles, leaf veins, wear, or functional geometry.

OBJECT-TO-CREATURE TRANSFORMATION — MOST IMPORTANT
The result must look like a real creature born from the object, not an object
with generic eyes, arms, and legs pasted onto it. Preserve the object's
silhouette logic and strongest physical features, but simplify tiny details.

Choose a body plan that naturally follows this object's geometry. It may be
bipedal, quadrupedal, serpentine, shelled, floating, rolling, rooted, winged,
many-legged, or amorphous. Zero arms, zero legs, or neither is fully valid.
Never add hands merely so the creature can gesture or feet merely so it can
stand. Do not default every object to a mascot, robot, or cyborg anatomy.

Keep shapes simple, readable, and strong at mobile-game size.

COLOR
The object's dominant colors occupy most of the creature. Use darker versions
for shadows, lighter versions for highlights, and at most 1–2 compact accent
colors. Accent colors follow anatomy or material boundaries and never form an
emblem or logo-like isolated mark.

WHITE IS NOT A GENERIC ACCENT
If the photographed material is not naturally white or off-white, never paint
white or off-white highlights, stripes, slashes, holes, shine, or decorative
marks on it. Highlights must be a lighter version of that material's own hue.
White is allowed only for eye sclera, teeth, and real naturally white material
explicitly named in the object context.

CHARACTER CONSISTENCY
The seven character cells depict the exact SAME individual. Preserve identical
body proportions, facial structure, eye design, limb count including zero,
palette, material finish, accessories, anatomy, character direction, and
signature features. Only pose, expression, restrained accents, and damage state
may change. The two effect cells contain ONLY battle effects — never a creature.

HORIZONTAL FACING LOCK — HOME AND BATTLE CONTRACT
The source direction is fixed, not an artistic choice. In EVERY character cell,
the creature's face or equivalent leading sensory plane, head, torso, support
points, and whole-body posture face canvas-left (the viewer's left) in the same
forward-left three-quarter orientation. Never face the viewer head-on, sheet
center, a neighboring cell, or canvas-right.

The camera never orbits. The same flank remains nearer the camera in all seven
character cells. Never mirror, flip, turn around, swap an asymmetrical landmark,
or move a feature to the opposite anatomical side for a better composition.
Left-column cells Idle, Happy, and Damaged are the highest risk: they still face
canvas-left and never turn inward toward Battle or Hungry.

EYE GAZE LOCK — SAME TARGET IN EVERY OPEN-EYE POSE
In Idle, Battle, Happy, Hungry, Dirty, and every open-eye Damaged expression,
both pupils or equivalent sensory focus look toward ONE shared target in open
space at canvas-left, along the same direction as the face and body. Never look
at the viewer, toward sheet center, toward canvas-right, cross-eyed, wall-eyed,
or in two different directions. Preserve pupil size, iris design, highlight
placement, and left/right eye identity; eyelids and brows may change expression,
but pupil direction does not.

Sleep keeps every eye fully closed. Damaged may use tired half-lidded eyes, but
any visible pupils still target canvas-left. A one-eyed creature keeps that one
eye focused canvas-left. If the body plan has no visible eyes or conventional
face, its opening, sensor, front rim, feeler cluster, or strongest directional
landmark acts as the gaze and keeps the same canvas-left focus.

This calm leftward focus remains natural for a companion standing in Home and
also satisfies Battle: the client mirrors the complete sheet for a creature
fighting from the left side, so opponents face and look toward each other.

In the BATTLE cell, the wind-up, gaze, extended limb or object-derived tool,
body thrust, and motion accents attack toward canvas-left. A `projectile` or
`sweep` effect must also have a clear canvas-left direction. An `impact` or
`bloom` effect is centered and may be directionless; never add a comet tail to
make it look like a projectile.

COMPOSITION — EXACTLY NINE CELLS IN A 3x3 ARRANGEMENT
Keep the same camera: three-quarter view from slightly above, facing
forward-left. Full body visible in every character cell at comparable scale.

Treat every cell as an invisible sealed safe box. Keep every body part,
appendage, cable, tail, motion line, spark, dust puff, tiny debris fragment, Z,
and battle effect at least 12% of that cell's width and height away from every
internal seam. Nothing from one cell may enter or appear inside another cell.
The post-process rejects the entire sheet rather than guessing fragment
ownership when this safe envelope is violated.

Detached accents ARE allowed and encouraged where the pose calls for them, but
they must remain compact, visually clustered near their own character or effect,
and wholly inside the safe envelope. Nothing may be cropped. No panel borders,
grid lines, or cell labels.

MIDDLE LEFT — HAPPY

VFX DIVERSITY CONTRACT
The two bottom effect cells must look unmistakably born from THIS object's
structural features, real material, and named moves. They must differ in overall
silhouette, topology, and animation logic. Never make Special merely a larger
or brighter version of Attack.

Never default to a round fireball, energy orb, comet, or generic explosion. A
closed ball is allowed only if the photographed object's real geometry or
function is itself spherical or launches a ball. Do not add a travel tail to
`impact` or `bloom`.

Motion-specific composition:
- projectile: compact directional form with one readable travel tail
- sweep: long crescent, ribbon, tread, blade, or whip form spanning sideways
- impact: centered contact mark, stamp, crack, slash, or shatter; no travel tail
- bloom: centered radial, branching, cloudy, ring-like, or erupting growth; no tail

BACKGROUND — TECHNICAL TRANSPORT LAYER
The entire canvas background must be solid, flat, perfectly uniform chroma key
green #00FF00, RGB (0,255,0). No gradient, noise, texture, floor, shadow, glow,
scenery, props, panel borders, or grid lines. If the object is naturally green,
use darker, lighter, or less saturated object greens, never exact #00FF00.

NEGATIVE SPACE — MUST REMAIN BACKGROUND
Every true opening, hole, cutout, split, handle gap, ring center, arch, or other
negative space must be exact chroma background #00FF00. Never fill or outline
negative space with white, off-white, gray, or a painted highlight.

For Monstera and every fenestrated leaf, each fenestration is a literal hole,
never a white stripe or leaf marking. Fill it with exact #00FF00.

Before finishing, inspect every white shape inside the creature. Remove it

EDGES — DARK CONTOUR DIRECTLY AGAINST GREEN
The moderately bold dark contour is the final outer edge and touches chroma

separators, or outlines around or inside the creature or battle effects.

same canvas-left point; none looks at the viewer, center, right, or a different
target. Do not submit the sheet until both checks pass.
