GLOBAL STYLE LOCK — IDENTICAL FOR EVERY SCANIMA
2D Japanese anime creature character design with the clear silhouettes, broad
character range, and expressive readability of late-1990s monster games, while
remaining an entirely original character that does not copy or closely
resemble an existing franchise design.

Polished game character illustration. Clean confident anime linework, moderately
bold dark graphic contours, simplified stylized forms, strong readable
silhouette, object-led proportions, slightly exaggerated anatomy where anatomy
exists, and an expressive anime creature face.

Use flat base colors, crisp 2–3 level cel shading, hard-edged anime shadows,
small controlled highlights, and minimal gradients. The image must look clearly
hand-illustrated in 2D, never rendered as CGI.

Techno-organic, robotic, armored, wired, or mechanical details are appropriate
ONLY when the photographed object is itself electronic or mechanical. Organic,
ceramic, glass, fabric, wood, paper, food, and plant objects must retain their
own material language instead of being converted into robots or cyborgs.

CHARACTER RANGE — FOLLOW THE OBJECT
Treat the character direction above as part of the creature's identity. Express

The creature may read as cute, feminine, masculine, androgynous or neutral,

Do not default to fierce, angry, masculine, cute, or childlike. Do not invent

SURFACE MARKS — OMIT, NEVER REPLACE
The reference photograph may carry brand logos, wordmarks, swooshes,

lettering, or other emblem-like graphics. Treat every such mark as absent from

OBJECT-TO-CREATURE TRANSFORMATION — MOST IMPORTANT
The result must look like a real creature born from the object, not an object
with generic eyes, arms, and legs pasted onto it. Preserve the object's
silhouette logic and strongest physical features, but simplify tiny surface
details.

The object's parts do NOT all need to become literal anatomy. Biological eyes,
mouths, teeth, paws, claws, or limbs are allowed only when they strengthen this
specific design. Object parts may instead become armor plates only when rigid,
brow shapes, crests without symbols, scales, tails, weapons, or accessories.

Choose a body plan that naturally follows this object's geometry and the visual
transformation above. It may be bipedal, quadrupedal, serpentine, shelled,
floating, rolling, rooted, winged, many-legged, or amorphous. Zero arms, zero
legs, or neither is fully valid. Never add hands merely so the creature can
gesture, and never add feet merely so it can stand. Do not default every object
to the same quadruped, insect, mascot, robot, or cyborg anatomy.

Keep shapes simple, readable, and strong at mobile-game size. Cute qualities,
confidence, elegance, toughness, and other character traits must follow the
chosen character direction instead of being forced into every creature.

COLOR
The object's dominant colors occupy most of the creature. Use darker versions
for shadows, lighter versions for highlights, and at most 1–2 compact accent
colors for personality or elemental details. Accent colors must follow anatomy
or material boundaries, never form an emblem or logo-like isolated mark. Do not
automatically make every creature black, neon, or rainbow-colored.

WHITE IS NOT A GENERIC ACCENT
If the photographed material is not naturally white or off-white, never paint
white or off-white highlights, stripes, slashes, holes, shine, or decorative
marks on it. Highlights must be a lighter version of that material's own hue.
White is allowed only for eye sclera, teeth, and real naturally white material
explicitly named in the object context.

CHARACTER CONSISTENCY
The seven character cells depict the exact SAME individual. Preserve identical

direction, and object-derived signature features. Only pose, expression,
restrained effects, and damage state may change. The two effect cells contain
ONLY the attack effect — never the creature, never a second body.

HORIZONTAL FACING LOCK — BATTLE CONTRACT
The facing direction is fixed, not an artistic choice. In EVERY character cell,
the creature must face canvas-left (the viewer's left) in the same forward-left
three-quarter orientation. Its face or leading front plane, nose or equivalent
front landmark, torso, feet or support points, and posture must all agree.

Never mirror, flip, turn around, or face canvas-right in any single cell,
including Sleep and Damaged. Keep every asymmetrical structural landmark on the
same anatomical side across all character cells; never swap left and right
merely to improve a pose composition. If the body plan has no face, use its
leading edge, opening, controls, tail attachment, or strongest asymmetrical
recognition anchor to preserve the same forward-left orientation.

In the BATTLE cell, the wind-up, gaze, extended limb or object-derived tool,
